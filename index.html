About Noad

Noad is a web browser with media discovery, downloads, ad blocking, and casting to DLNA and Google Cast receivers. Noad has no app account, publisher-operated browsing server, advertising SDK, analytics service, or automatic crash-reporting service. It does not upload your browsing database to the publisher. Browsing, suggestions, filter downloads, and casting do contact other devices and services.

Websites and searches

Websites and their embedded services receive requests needed to load content. These can include your IP address, URLs, browser information, cookies, and information you enter or upload. Their privacy practices apply. Ad blocking cannot prevent every form of tracking.

Submitting a search sends the search text to your selected engine: DuckDuckGo, Google, Bing, or Brave Search. Following a link, opening a saved page, or restoring the selected tab also contacts the website. Other restored tabs load when selected.

Address-bar suggestions

Search suggestions are enabled by default. While enabled, non-address text of at least two characters in the address bar is sent over HTTPS to DuckDuckGo for autocomplete, before you submit a search and also in private tabs. This happens regardless of your selected search engine. DuckDuckGo receives the query and connection information such as your IP address. Noad's suggestion client does not attach browsing cookies, a referrer, or a Noad account identifier.

Turn Search suggestions off in Settings to stop new autocomplete requests. Requests already sent may finish, and other browsing/network activity can continue. A small cache of queries and results remains in app memory until replaced or the process ends. DuckDuckGo's privacy policy is at https://duckduckgo.com/privacy.

Ad-blocking filters

When ad blocking initializes, Noad downloads EasyList and Fanboy's Annoyance List from easylist.to over HTTPS. That host and its hosting providers receive your IP address and the filter-list URL. Noad does not send your browsing history with these downloads. Filter matching and cosmetic filtering run on your device.

Turning Block ads and trackers off disables filtering and prevents new filter initialization through page setup. Work already queued with Android's background scheduler may finish or retry, including after Noad closes. Downloaded lists remain on the device. The filter-list host's retention and processing are outside the publisher's control.

Media verification and casting

Opening Sources, a saved source group, or Recent sources can check multiple media addresses automatically; Recent sources checks only the rows on screen. These requests reveal your IP address and the media URL to its host, including any token embedded in the URL. Noad's verification client does not add browsing cookies, an authorization header, or a referrer.

Opening Devices starts local-network discovery. Devices on that network can see discovery traffic; Noad reads receiver descriptions and identifiers. Choosing a receiver sends the media URL and playback metadata to it. Before sending to a DLNA receiver, Noad asks it over the local network which formats it accepts. Playback controls send commands and receive status. These operations can continue during a casting session after the picker closes.

When a receiver refuses to play a stream, Noad checks why by fetching the start of that stream the way the receiver would. It requests the media address and, for a playlist, at most one further playlist and the first kilobyte of one segment. The playlist names those addresses, so they can be on a different host. These requests reveal your IP address and those addresses, including any token embedded in them, to their hosts. They carry no browsing cookies, authorization header, or referrer. For a Google Cast receiver they name the Cast receiver's web origin (www.gstatic.com), as the receiver itself does. Nothing from this check is stored.

Before sending an HLS stream to a Google Cast receiver, Noad reads its playlist, and for a master playlist one variant playlist, to tell the receiver whether the stream is live and what kind of segments it uses. For a finished stream it also reads the first 32 kilobytes of the second segment, to learn whether the segments start on a keyframe. These requests are made the same way as the check above. Before sending an HLS stream to a DLNA receiver, Noad requests its playlist once the same way, with no origin. If the site refuses that request, or, for Google Cast, answers without the permission the receiver needs to read it, Noad asks again the way the page did: naming as referrer and origin only the web origin that requested the stream while you watched (for example https://example.com/, never a page's path) — the page itself or the video player embedded in it — or else the origin of the page the stream was found on, or the stream's own origin. The site already received that origin when the page played the stream.

When the segments do not start on a keyframe, or when the site serves the stream to the page but not to the receiver, Noad relays the stream: as the receiver asks for it, the phone fetches the playlists and segments from the site and passes them to the receiver from a temporary web server on your local network — regrouped so each starts on a keyframe in the first case, unchanged in the second. The site then receives those requests from your phone, revealing your IP address, Noad's user agent and the addresses, including any token in them, instead of receiving them from the receiver. No cookies or authorization header are sent. A stream relayed because the site refused the receiver is requested with the page's origin as referrer and origin, as described above; otherwise no referrer is sent. The server listens only on the network address that reaches that receiver, only while relaying, and answers only addresses containing a random token that Noad gives the receiver; anyone on your network who learns such an address could fetch the same stream until relaying ends. Regrouped segments are held in memory, at most 64 megabytes, and discarded when relaying ends; segments passed on unchanged are held only while they are handed over. Nothing is written to storage. While relaying, Android shows a notification where notifications are allowed, Noad keeps the phone's processor and Wi-Fi awake, and Stop in that notification ends it. Relaying ends with the cast, or after 45 minutes without a request.

Otherwise the receiver fetches internet media itself. Its manufacturer and media service have their own privacy practices. DLNA discovery and control are unencrypted. Google Cast control uses TLS. Noad asks the receiver to prove it holds a device certificate issued under Google's Cast certificate authority, tells you whether it could, and sends nothing until you choose to trust that device; a genuine Cast device is not necessarily yours, and a receiver that cannot prove itself is identified only by the certificate fingerprint you approve. Use a receiver and network you trust.

Downloads, uploads, and sharing

Downloads start after confirmation and use Android's Download Manager. The service receives the file URL and browser information, and can receive the referring site's origin (its scheme and host, never the page's path or query). Noad first follows the file's redirects itself, sending each address only that site's own cookies; when the chain stays on the requested site, the actual download starts from where the chain ended and receives cookies for that site. Downloads can continue after Noad closes, including over mobile data or roaming. Public Downloads files can remain after uninstall.

Uploads give the website access to files you select through Android's picker. A capture-enabled upload can offer the camera after Android camera permission is granted. Completed photos/videos are passed to the requesting page; canceled captures are discarded. Successful temporary captures may remain in Noad's private cache until a later chooser cleanup removes files older than a day, Android removes the cache, or app storage is cleared. The camera app or file provider has its own privacy practices.

Sharing passes the page URL to your chosen app. Sharing a video source passes that source's media address and title to the app you pick; Noad sends no cookies or other headers with it. Copying puts selected text or links on Android's clipboard. Opening the address bar reads an available clipboard link to offer a paste suggestion; reading it alone does not send it to autocomplete. Android and the chosen apps control their handling of shared data.

Camera, microphone, and location

Live website camera, microphone, or location requests show a Noad prompt naming the requesting origin and access. Only the selected tab can raise such requests. Allowing access lets Noad request missing Android permissions. Android denial prevents access; approximate location permission is sufficient if you choose it. Camera access for an upload uses the separate picker flow described above.

Website Allow and Block choices are saved locally for future visits, across restarts. Review or forget them in Settings → Site permissions. When a page opens a pop-up or sends you to another website on its own, Noad holds it and records your answer against that site; review or forget those in Settings → Pop-ups and redirects. Neither kind of answer given in a private tab is saved, and a site embedded in another page is asked every time rather than answered from, or saved into, that record. Neither record leaves the device. Clearing Cookies and site data also forgets them. Forgetting affects future requests; reload or close a page to stop access it already holds. You can revoke Noad's permissions in Android Settings. Pending site requests are canceled when their tab changes, navigates, or closes. Noad requests no background-location permission and runs no location-tracking service.

Local records and backups

Noad stores history, typed entries, bookmarks, folders, saved source groups, recently cast media sources (their addresses, names, the page each came from, and the device it played on; never from a private tab), ordinary tabs, download records, site permissions, pop-up and redirect answers, trusted Cast fingerprints, and preferences in app-private storage. It caches site icons, ordinary-tab previews, filter lists, and temporary captures. WebView stores cookies, website data, and cache. Site-icon lookups use a local cache rather than a separate favicon service.

If Android cloud backup or device transfer is enabled, Noad's rules include only bookmarks. The backup/transfer service can receive your bookmarks; its settings, security, retention, and restore behavior apply. History, tabs, source groups, recent sources, permissions, pop-up and redirect answers, receiver trust, and WebView browsing data are excluded by Noad's rules. This policy does not control copies you export or services operated by Android or a device manufacturer.

Retention and deletion

Local records remain until deleted, replaced under Noad's storage limits, or app storage is cleared. Settings → Clear browsing data offers history, cookies/site data, WebView cache, and tabs. Recent-history deletion uses the selected time range; all-history deletion also clears typed entries and stored site icons. Cookies, website data, and WebView cache are cleared across all sites regardless of time range.

Recently closed ordinary tabs (address, title, and position) are kept in memory only, so Reopen closed tab can bring them back; they are discarded when the app process ends, and clearing history clears them for the same time range. Private tabs are never kept.

Manage bookmarks and saved source groups in their own screens. Clear browsing data does not remove these or downloaded files. Downloads has separate controls for download records and files. Remove trusted receivers in Settings. Android Clear storage or uninstall removes app-private records, but not necessarily public downloads or older backups. Manage those through Android and your backup provider. Noad cannot erase data already received by websites, search services, receivers, or apps you selected; contact those recipients for deletion options.

Private tabs

Private tabs do not add history or typed-entry records, save their previews/site icons, or restore after restart. They bypass the WebView cache when loading pages and refuse third-party cookies; what they fetch can still be written to the shared WebView cache until the private session is cleared. They still use the same network, suggestion setting, shared WebView cookie/storage profile, and site-permission system as ordinary tabs. Bookmarks, source groups you save, downloads, temporary uploads, and permission choices are not covered by a promise of leaving no records.

Closing the last private tab that loaded a page clears cookies, website data, WebView cache, saved site permissions, saved pop-up and redirect answers, and certificate warnings you chose to continue past, for the whole app. The same clearing runs when you leave Noad with a private tab open, and — if Noad was closed from recent apps, crashed, or the phone restarted while a private tab was open — the next time Noad starts, before any page loads. This can sign you out of ordinary tabs. Private browsing does not conceal activity from websites, receivers, your network provider, or services receiving requests.

Connection security and Android services

Noad uses HTTPS for autocomplete and its configured filter addresses. As a browser and DLNA client, it also supports unencrypted HTTP websites and media/control endpoints. Not every connection is encrypted. Continuing past a certificate warning weakens verification for that site until the tab where you chose it is closed, and no tab shows that site as secure in the meantime.

WebView Safe Browsing is enabled where supported. Installed WebView and Android providers may contact safety services according to their implementation/settings. Noad opts out of WebView usage statistics. This does not disable WebView crash reporting: if you enable sharing diagnostics with Google in Android, WebView may send crash information and an app-specific diagnostic token to Google. Noad sends no automatic analytics or crash reports to its publisher.

Android also provides downloads, background scheduling, permission dialogs, file selection, and backup/transfer. These follow your platform/provider settings and policies. Google's WebView reporting explanation is at https://developer.android.com/develop/ui/views/layout/webapps/webview-privacy.

Children and changes

Noad provides general web access and is not designed specifically for children. Websites can contain unsuitable content. Changes to Noad's data handling will be reflected in this policy, with a new effective date, and in release information. Questions about this policy can be sent to iwikpro@gmail.com.
